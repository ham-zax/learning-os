# Pedagogical priorities for Learning OS

Research date: 2026-10-01. This is a research note and proposed evaluation policy, not an accepted kernel contract or evidence that Learning OS improves learning.

The highest-value next step is a longitudinal pilot that measures delayed, unassisted transfer, alongside making existing teaching policies concrete enough to apply consistently. Learning OS already distinguishes assistance from clean retrieval and already prescribes worked examples, scaffold withdrawal, selective feedback, reconstruction, variants and practical work. Merely adding another rule to “space practice” or “ask questions” would miss the remaining question: which sequence helps this learner independently solve unfamiliar programming problems efficiently?

## What the current repository establishes

These are verified implementation facts, separate from the research findings below:

- [Teacher pedagogy](../../src/teacher-pedagogy.ts) supplies a binary guided/independent starting directive. New objectives, pending diagnostics, due retrieval and transfer start independently. [The teacher protocol](../teacher-agent-protocol.md) explicitly allows prior-knowledge-sensitive scaffolding, teaching first when prerequisites are missing, concise repair and withdrawal of help. The gap is consistent execution and curated progressions, not the absence of a scaffolding policy.
- [Prediction calibration](../../src/knowledge/challenge-calibration.ts) defines executable same/variant/transfer cases, expected outputs and contrasting wrong models, specifically for `predict`/`runtime_trace`. This is a concrete content pattern to adapt for curated debugging and implementation activities; these research notes do not expand the kernel's task contracts.
- [Dogfood metrics](../../src/evaluation/dogfood-metrics.ts) count delayed valid retrievals, correct transfers, repair exposures, reconstructions and abandoned episodes. They do not report baseline-adjusted learning gain, transfer opportunities or active-time-normalized effectiveness. [Teacher evaluation](../teacher-evaluation.md) explicitly distinguishes synthetic lifecycle checks from a longitudinal learner pilot.

Existing teacher guidance already includes contrasting cases and targeted feedback. Recommended changes should improve their authoring, calibration and observed execution, rather than add mandatory questioning or a separate mastery/scheduling model.

## Prioritized improvements to evaluate

1. **Make acquisition and scaffold withdrawal observable.** For two high-use objectives, curate a small progression: subgoal-labelled example, partially completed solution, independent implementation or debugging, then an unfamiliar transfer case. Include one brief prompt about why a consequential step works. Existing policy already permits this; the improvement is reusable examples and explicit decisions about which scaffold to remove next. Use demonstrated performance to choose guidance rather than self-rated expertise. Keep assisted steps distinct from clean evidence. The subgoal and fading findings motivate this candidate sequence; they do not establish its optimal length.

2. **Test discrimination, not just different-looking repetitions.** After a learner can execute each component, mix cases that require choosing among confusable mechanisms. For instance, vary whether a closure observes a mutated object, a reassigned binding, or a captured value; ask for the decisive distinction before tracing output. Remove chapter/topic cues from assessment cases. Existing variants can be audited for whether they change the required reasoning or merely rename variables. The interleaving evidence motivates selective contrasts, not random rotation through unrelated topics.

3. **Consolidate the learner's actual failed model.** Where prerequisites support meaningful exploration, permit a short attempt or alternative designs before instruction, then connect the explanation to the learner's solution and its limits. When the learner cannot interpret the task or generate a meaningful approach, use a worked example first. After repair, test the same principle in a new case after a delay. Productive-failure research supports this conditional design; it does not support repeatedly withholding help until failure.

4. **Audit assistance for future independence.** Track whether hint requirements decrease across matched unseen tasks and whether learners can explain and execute a repaired solution later. Include concise feedback for sound but uncertain answers, not only errors. A reveal followed immediately by paraphrasing is useful guided practice, but cannot substitute for delayed independent performance. The feedback and AI findings motivate these outcome checks; the kernel's existing exposure rules should continue to own classification and scheduling.

5. **Run the outcome pilot before widening policy.** Synthetic teacher scenarios and read-only dogfood counts can verify behavior and routing. They cannot establish retention or transfer. Start with a small number of objectives and one policy comparison, keeping challenge difficulty, assessment rubric and teaching time comparable. Avoid simultaneously changing all five capabilities and every delivery context.

## Pilot measures

The proposed primary outcome is **delayed unassisted performance on unfamiliar tasks**, adjusted for baseline ability. Report the corresponding gain per active study minute alongside raw pretest/posttest scores, task difficulty and minutes; a ratio alone can reward learners who already knew the material or hide weak absolute performance. Use equivalent unseen baseline and delayed cases, and freeze rubrics before teaching. Assess after roughly one week; add a later follow-up where feasible. Those intervals are pilot choices, not replacements for FSRS due dates. Assess executable tasks using real tests and score reasoning separately when the objective requires it.

Use parallel cases rather than repeating a memorized snippet. Record hint depth, reveal frequency, independent task completion, recurring misconception, time to viable approach, frustration and return rate as secondary measures. Distinguish active study time from calendar time, tool execution and conversation delays; obtain modest learner time estimates if reliable instrumentation is unavailable. Report misses and non-returners rather than treating absence as learning success.

A within-learner comparison across matched objective pairs can provide an initial signal, but prior knowledge, order effects and cross-topic transfer can confound it. Counterbalance sequence where feasible. A single-learner pilot estimates usefulness for that learner, not a population effect. Do not promote a policy because assisted accuracy or learner satisfaction rises alone. Adopt it when delayed independent performance improves with acceptable time and persistence; otherwise revise the task, guidance or assessment before expanding.

For one learner, start with matched unfamiliar objective families and compare the present teaching policy with one curated example/completion/fading sequence. Keep total study time comparable. With multiple learners, randomize a single policy comparison and account for repeated attempts from the same learner; do not treat every attempt as an independent participant. Prespecify the outcome and report uncertainty and non-returners. A small pilot is a feasibility and direction check, not sufficient evidence for a population-level effectiveness claim.

## Additional programming-specific checks

[Hou, Ericson & Wang (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.18115v1) randomized 89 undergraduates to code-writing practice with or without Parsons scaffolding (ordering provided code blocks). Lower-self-efficacy learners improved practice performance and efficiency with scaffolding, but the study found no significant posttest difference. This motivates testing a completion/ordering bridge where learners are stuck; it does not justify counting scaffold completion as independent implementation or promising retention gains.

A recent [ESSE self-explanation tutor preprint, September 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25180v2), reports richer explanations and revision behavior in a Java-course pilot. Its learning deployment involved eight students, with only three completing the posttest; it cannot establish robust learning gains. Human comparisons also identified grader disagreements. The practical recommendation is to audit actual tutor judgments against human-rated correct, mistaken and ambiguous explanations before relying on model feedback to adapt instruction. Evaluate conceptual correctness separately from answer length and fluency.
